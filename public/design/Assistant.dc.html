Three things stand out. Payment is net 45 from invoice receipt (§4.2), with 1.5% monthly interest on late amounts — slower than the net 30 you usually sign.

Termination: either party may terminate for convenience on 90 days' written notice (§11.1), but Northwind can terminate for cause on 15 days if you miss two consecutive payments (§11.3). There's no wind-down or transition-assistance obligation, which is worth adding.

Liability is capped at the fees paid in the 12 months preceding the claim (§9.2), with the usual carve-outs for IP indemnity, confidentiality breach, and gross negligence. Consequential damages are mutually waived.

Two clauses I'd flag beyond your question: §7.4 gives them a unilateral right to change subprocessors with notice only, and §13.2 sets Delaware venue with no arbitration step.

follow-up · is a 12-month fee cap unusual at ~$340k/yr? judgment task — routed to human review

For a $340k annual contract, a 12-month fee cap is common but on the vendor-friendly side of normal. At that spend, buyers with leverage typically land somewhere between 1× and 2× annual fees, and often carve data-breach liability out of the cap entirely rather than folding it in.

The part I'd push on isn't the multiple — it's that §9.2 measures the cap on fees paid, not fees payable. In year one that could leave you with a cap of only a few months' spend if something goes wrong early.

A reasonable ask: 1.5× trailing twelve months, measured on fees payable, with a separate super-cap for security incidents.
